# Product

## What it is
GamePlan is a laptop tool for baseball hitting development. For a lineup against a specific starter it shows, per hitter and count, where to swing and where to take, whether the hitter can do damage on what he swings at, and how his swing path fits the pitch. After the game it reviews every pitch against that plan.

## Mode
Operate. Coaches and analysts use it to complete a task, so scanability, stable density and consistency outrank expression.

## Users
Primary: hitting coaches in a pregame series meeting and a post-game review, and player development staff and analysts working through many hitters and games. Secondary: hitters reviewing their own at-bats. Mostly a laptop, often with other staff watching.

## Voice
Plain, exact and brief, like a scouting report. Short labels first; a one-line explanation only where a number could be misread. No chat tone, no commentary about "him" doing things, no filler. Use baseball terms the staff already use.

## Visual direction
Club-branded for the Arizona Diamondbacks palette: Sedona red, black and Sonoran sand, with teal as a secondary accent. Light and dark themes. Calm and dense, instrument-like. Swing and take keep their own blue and red meaning, and club red is never used to mean "take" or "bad". No logos or marks (org approval is needed before this is shown as a club tool).

## Content rules
- Every number carries its sample size or is flagged as thin.
- Say once what a term means, in a tooltip or a short note, not in running prose.
- The plan says nothing on about half of pitches; the interface states that plainly instead of hiding it.

## Anti-references
Casual dashboards with paragraphs under every chart. Big-number tiles for their own sake. Decorative gradients, glows or icons standing in for content.
